## The excavation-pit defect — measured, not guessed

Mark: *"The scale is wrong on the pump; it looks too small against the background… by the
tracks and dirt, you can tell the pump is only a few feet long."*

He supplied a reference that used the same plate:
**`C:/Projects/work-assets/Renderings/DD6-Rendering-jobsite.png`** (1239×848), a DD6 pump in
this same excavation pit. His dimensions: the DD6 is **~110 in** overall, tow-tongue tip to
rearmost jack stand; the RL300's overall length is **approximately the same**, so *"the
length of the 300 should fill most of that image."*

Measured here:

| | Method | On-screen extent |
|---|---|---|
| DD6 in the reference | yellow-body column profile, columns with ≥12 yellow px, top 18% excluded so the background excavator cannot set the bound | x[130, 1185] = 1056 px = **85.2% of frame width** |
| RL300 in the current composite | exact, from the render's own alpha `mask.png` | x[201, 679] = 479 px = **53.2% of frame width** (376 px tall = 60.2% of height) |

**Naive ratio: 85.2 / 53.2 = 1.60×.** Treat that as a *floor*, not the answer, because the
two poses differ: the DD6 is near-broadside, so its 1056 px is close to its true 110 in,
while the RL300 sits at roughly three-quarters. A three-quarter box projects *wider* than
its length alone (≈ L·cos θ + W·sin θ), so a correctly-scaled RL300 at this pose should read
**at or above** 85.2%, plausibly ~90%. It is also genuinely taller than the DD6 (a tall
enclosure versus a low trailer), so expect it to fill much more height — at 1.6× it would be
~602 px in a 625 px frame, which is tight. Check the vertical before committing.

### Fix it with the camera, not `product_scale`

`compositing.product_scale` exists in `jobs/rl300_03_excavation-pit.json` (currently `1.0`),
but `composite_worker._place_product` applies it as a **LANCZOS resize of the already-rendered
product**. Upscaling 1.6× softens every product pixel — the wrong tool for a scale error, and
against the spirit of the Product Pixel Integrity Gate.

The job's camera block is `focal_length_mm: 85.0`, `distance_multiplier: 8.5`. Apparent size
goes as 1/distance, so as a **starting point**:

    distance_multiplier: 8.5 → ~5.3        (8.5 / 1.60)

Re-render, re-measure the mask bbox, iterate. Confirm first how `distance_multiplier` is
consumed in `msp_render_cli/cameras.py` — the linear assumption above is unverified.
