## What happened, and the two lessons in it

### v10 — the flange joint was never closed

Mark, looking at the v9 render: the bolts and washers did not move when the
fitting was replaced, and both were resting inside the flange.

He was right, and the gap was in `scripts/edit_rl300_geometry.py`'s own checks.
**Every placement gate was radial or rotational** — bolt circle to 3 µm, hole
phase to 0.12° — so a replacement flange of a different thickness passed all of
them while burying the hardware it was supposed to clamp. Nothing measured the
axial stack. My first diagnosis was wrong too: I read the space the misplaced
hardware left as the design requirement and concluded the flange was too thick.
Mark corrected me with the actual stack — 1.125 + 0.125 + 0.500 in — and that
was the key to it.

Four fixes, all in `seat_flange_hardware()` and `lengthen_bolts()`:

1. Raised face off. The gasket `V2GSK-FLG-A200-125-1` is full-face, reaching
   r=171.9 mm past the bolt circle at r=149.2 mm, so the 1/16 in boss at
   r=135.4 mm left the bolts clamping a 1.6 mm air gap.
2. Inboard washers and bolts seated 15.872 mm onto the flange back face.
3. Bolt heads seated on their washers — each sank 3.655 mm into its own washer
   in the source CAD, invisible while the stack was buried.
4. Bolts 2.75 → 3.25 in at Mark's direction, stretched across the plain shank
   only so thread pitch is carried rather than scaled.

The measured stack now closes with no gaps and no interference: 44.443 mm of
clamped material (1.7497 in) and 82.55 mm under-head (3.250 in). The full table
is in [the v10 record](docs/rl300-parity.md#2026-09-13--v10-flange-joint-rebuilt).

### v11 — an appearance fix that only ever reached one job

Mark, on the v10 anchor: the latches were supposed to be stainless.

They were never fixed in that render. The four `metal_finish.reassign` rules
added on 2026-09-12 went into `jobs/rl300_04_studio-white.json` **only**.
`00023779` and `reassign` have never appeared in `jobs/rl300_02_studio-dark.json`
in any commit. So the studio-dark anchor — the default job — plus
`rl300_01_no-background` and `rl300_03_excavation-pit` rendered the latch on
`MSP_PLASTIC` the whole time. The white proof was the only place the fix was
ever visible, which is why the before/after sheet looked right.

**Lesson worth carrying: an appearance fix put in one job manifest silently
misses the other three.** When you correct a material, check all of
`jobs/*.json`, or put it where every job sees it.

`correct_cad_materials()` now applies all four to the source. `reassign` is gone
from the white job; `metal_finish.materials` is added to the dark job so the
anchor gets the same turned-aluminium and stainless tuning.

## Facts worth not re-deriving

- **The fitting is no longer hand-authored into the .blend.** The script *runs*
  `cad/generators/flanged_camlock_800al.py`, so a dimension change is a one-line
  edit plus a replay and nobody has to open Blender. Mark's own copy at
  `C:/Projects/Misc/fitting.py` is byte-identical apart from a header and
  `SCALE_TO_METERS`; it is his, leave it alone.
- **Two traps that each cost a run** when wiring that up. The generator builds
  along local **+Z** with the flange face at z=0, so the part must be pre-oriented
  `Rx(+90)` and parked at `(0, 2, 0)` before `bake_barrel_to_local_y` sees it.
  And removing the previously authored object leaves its **mesh orphaned**, which
  keeps that mesh's material alive through the save — Blender's save-time purge is
  a single pass — surfacing as `SOURCE_STRUCTURE_MISMATCH`. Remove the mesh too.
- **`Mesh_203_LP` has exactly 16 users, all flange bolts.** That is why
  lengthening it in place is safe. `lengthen_bolts()` asserts the count and
  refuses otherwise.
- The eight auto-named glTF colour materials the pump carried are now unused and
  dropped on save: the blend went from 20 materials to 12.
- Compare references by **decoded-pixel digest, never file hash** — beauty.png's
  SHA drifts on the render clock alone.
- `missing_texture: blocked` in a parity run is the **expected negative control**,
  not a failure. Exit 0 is the thing to read.
