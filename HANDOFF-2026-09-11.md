### The reference photographs — and what they already told me

Both files Mark named exist and I have looked at them:

- `C:\Projects\work-assets\photograph-studio\rl-200-safe-back-iso-older-whtbkgrd.jpg`
  — professional photograph, white sweep.
- `C:\Projects\work-assets\Renderings\rl200-safe-iso-back-render-older-std-bkgrd.png`
  — the same photograph, background replaced with the dark studio.

Near-identical package (previous revision; differs only in some sheet-metal
slots), and the second one sits in the *same* environment the pipeline is
targeting. It is as close to a ground truth as this project will get. The wider
folders hold many more RL200/DD4/DD6 studio shots worth mining.

**Two things fell out of comparing them to our render. Both matter more than
orange peel, and the second one contradicts the framing of the original
complaint — check my work before building on it.**

**1. The real photograph shows no visible orange peel either.** At full-machine
framing the genuine powder-coated RL200 reads as a smooth even yellow with soft
broad gradients. This corroborates the measured finding from this session: peel
is a 1–2 mm feature, the proof image gets ~4 mm/px and production ~2 mm/px, so
it is sub-pixel and cannot appear. Proven by cranking the same shader to 16.7 mm
/ strength 1.0 (obvious stucco) versus 1.5 mm (invisible), and by rendering
undenoised at 256 samples, which does not recover it. The peel stays in the
shader at honest values for close crops. **Do not spend the next session trying
to make orange peel visible at this framing.**

**2. The dominant gap is chroma, not micro-surface.** Sampling saturated-yellow
panel pixels (R>G>B, R>90), mean over the large rear panel:

| Source | Mean RGB | Mean HSV sat | Value SD |
|---|---|---|---|
| Photo, white bkgrd | (221, 166, **49**) | 200 | 25.3 |
| Photo, dark bkgrd (our environment) | (161, 99, **2**) | 251 | 24.9 |
| Our v5 render | (224, 186, **116**) | 125 | 25.7 |

Our yellow is roughly half as saturated, with a blue channel of 116 against 2–49
in the real thing. Brightness and tonal spread across the panel are already in
the right range (val_sd 25.7 vs 24.9), so the panel-to-panel falloff is fine —
it is the colour that is wrong.

I checked whether my coat layer caused it: v4 (no coat) sampled sat 122.8, v5
(coat) 125.1, so the coat very slightly *increased* saturation. The
desaturation is pre-existing and structural. The three candidates, in the order
I would test them:

1. **The AgX view transform** (`output.color.view_transform: AgX`, look
   `AgX - Medium High Contrast`). AgX attenuates chroma hard, especially as
   values climb. This is my first suspect and the cheapest to test — render the
   same frame through Standard/Filmic and re-sample.
2. **Base colour.** `[1.0, 0.56, 0.0]` should be a saturated amber; measure what
   it actually becomes after transform before changing it.
3. **Light colour/intensity.** Bright near-white key washing chroma out of the
   upper panel range.

Resolve chroma before touching anything else. If the yellow is wrong, no amount
of surface work will make the render read as the real machine, and Mark will
keep seeing "computer generated" for a reason that has nothing to do with the
finish.
